How the score is built

Scoring methodology

Bodie–Kane–Marcus, Investments 5e, Ch. 6

The tally

Each answer is weighted by its letter: a → 1, b → 2, c → 3. Your score is the plain sum across all scored items. Because the item ladders from the safest choice (a) to the most risk-seeking (c), the total is monotone in risk tolerance.

Why nine items, not seven

Question 2 re-poses the 20%-loss scenario across three time horizons — five, fifteen, and thirty years (items 2A, 2B, 2C) — and each is scored separately. That gives 9 scored items: Q1, 2A, 2B, 2C, Q3, Q4, Q5, Q6, Q7. The all-a floor is 9 × 1 = 9 and the all-c ceiling is 9 × 3 = 27, which is exactly why the published bands run from 9 to 27.
