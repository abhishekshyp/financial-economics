Framework · Anatomy of a rule04

Three decisions define every rebalancing rule

Portfolio rebalancing is the act of buying and selling assets to bring the portfolio's weights back toward the target asset allocation after market movements have changed them.

Decision 1

Frequency

How often weights are checked: daily, monthly, quarterly, yearly.

Decision 2

Threshold

How much drift is tolerated before a trade, e.g. ±5 pp around 33.33%.

Decision 3

Target weights

Where trades take the portfolio: back to target, halfway, or to the band edge.

Family 1 · Calendar-based05

Rebalance on a fixed schedule

Mechanics. On each scheduled date (e.g. the last trading day of the quarter), compute portfolio value V, then set every asset to V/N. Between dates, weights drift freely.

Pros

Simple rule; predictable dates; no monitoring between dates.

Con

Trades on every date, regardless of how far weights have actually drifted.

Example: quarterly rebalancing

Family 2 · Threshold-based06

Rebalance only when a band is breached

Mechanics. Check weights at every daily close. If any weight lies outside target ± X, set every asset back to V/N. Otherwise, no trade.

Band (X = 5 pp): 28.33% ≤ wi ≤ 38.33%

Pro

Trading is tied to actual deviation from target.

Con

Requires daily monitoring; impractical for most individuals.

Combination · Calendar + threshold07

Check on schedule, trade only on breach

Mechanics. Weights are checked only on scheduled dates (e.g. each quarter-end). A trade occurs only if, on that date, any weight lies outside the band. Breaches between scheduled dates are ignored.
