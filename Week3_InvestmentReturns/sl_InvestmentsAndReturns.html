The idea02

You will save a great deal over a lifetime. That money has to go somewhere.

In Week 1 we saw savings pile up across a working life. Left idle, they don't stay still — inflation quietly erodes them. Investment puts those savings to work.

Portfolio investment is money committed to stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs and other financial assets — or any asset with economic value that can produce future financial benefits.

Investment does two jobs at once: build wealth for life's goals, and fight inflation to protect purchasing power.
The two jobs of an investment
Job 01

Grow wealth

Turn today's surplus into tomorrow's house, education and retirement — the aspirations from your Week-1 life plan.

Job 02

Beat inflation

At ~6% inflation, ₹100 today buys about ₹55 in a decade. An investment must out-earn that erosion just to stand still.

Real vs financial assets03

Every asset is either real, or a claim on something real.

The material wealth of a society is set by the productive capacity of its economy — the goods and services it can create. That capacity rests on real assets: land, buildings, machines, knowledge.

Financial assets — stocks and bonds — are claims on the income those real assets generate. You may not own an auto plant, but you can buy shares in Tata Motors and share in the income from making cars.

Real assets produce the economy's income. Financial assets distribute it.
Real asset Financial asset
Nature Physical, productive A claim on income
Generates Goods & services Cash flows (rent, coupon, dividend)
Net wealth? Yes — society's wealth Nets to zero (asset = a liability)
Liquidity Usually low Usually high

Category What it is Indian examples

Where shares are born, and where they trade10

Primary market: the IPO. Secondary market: everything after.

Primary market — the IPO

A company offers shares to the public for the first time — an Initial Public Offer. Investors subscribe at the issue price; the money goes to the company.

Why go public? Mostly to raise funds for capex. Also to repay high-cost debt, or to give early investors an exit.

Secondary market — the exchange

Once listed, shares trade between investors on an electronic marketplace. The company gets nothing further — buyers and sellers simply exchange views, expressed as orders.

The IPO works because the secondary market exists: nobody buys a share they can't later sell.
Different views make a market11

Infosys at ₹3,000 — two opposing views meet

Trader A · SELL

"No new CEO in sight — it'll fall further." So A offers to sell at ₹3,000.

Trader B · BUY

"An overreaction — a great leader will come." So B bids to buy at ₹3,000.

The exchange matches the trade

Orders go broker → exchange. When a buy and a sell agree on price, the exchange matches them and the trade executes.

TRADE EXECUTED
1 INFY @ ₹3,000
A delivers the share · B pays ₹3,000 · last traded price = ₹3,000
A stock market simply lets opposing views transact. Different opinions are what make a market.

Measuring what you get back13

Investment returns — from a single change to an annualised number

rabs = (P₁ − P₀) / P₀

E[r] = (1/n) Σ rt

μann = μperiod × N

σann = σperiod × √N

N = periods per year: 252 trading days, 52 weeks, 12 months. Mean scales with time; risk scales with √time — the crucial asymmetry.
