The money didn't vanish. It changed hands.

In FY24, proprietary traders made about ₹33,000 crore. Foreign portfolio investors made about ₹28,000 crore.

Individuals and others lost about ₹61,000 crore.

33,000 + 28,000 ≈ 61,000

Every option bought is an option sold. Before costs, one side's gain is exactly the other side's loss.

This is not a coincidence. It is arithmetic, and William Sharpe wrote it down in 1991.
Part 2 · Sharpe (1991)05

The arithmetic of active management

Split all investors into two groups. Passive investors simply hold the whole market. Active investors are everyone else. Together, they own the market.

RM = w·RP + (1 − w)·RA together they hold the market
RP = RM passive holds the market
⇒ RA = RM before costs
⇒ RA − cA < RP − cP after costs

Active investors pay more in costs, so on average they must earn less than passive investors.

No theory needed

This is accounting, not a model of prices. It holds whether markets are efficient or not.

Some can win, but not all

An active investor can beat the market only if another active investor loses by the same amount.

F&O is even harsher

Derivatives are zero-sum by design. There is no market return to share, only costs to pay.

A fair caveat

Passive funds still trade at index changes, so the identity is not exact (Pedersen, 2018). It still holds broadly.

Part 3 · Barber & Odean (2000)07

The more they traded, the less they earned

Barber and Odean studied 66,465 US households at a large discount broker from 1991 to 1996. They saw every trade, so they could measure returns before and after costs.

The average household replaced 75% of its portfolio every year. The most active fifth replaced more than 250%. Each round trip cost about 4%.

Stock picking was not the problem. Before costs, heavy and light traders earned about the same. Trading costs made the difference.

Part 3 · Why trading hurts08

Three reasons, seen in the US and in India

Net return = Market return + Skill − (Turnover × Cost per round trip)
1 · Costs are certain

You always pay. You only sometimes win.

US: about 4% per round trip in the 1990s.
India: individuals paid about ₹50,000 crore in F&O costs over FY22–FY24, about ₹26,000 per trader in FY24.

2 · More trades, more losses

Frequency multiplies the damage.

US: the most active fifth earned 11.4% vs 18.5% for the least active.
India: among intraday traders with 500+ trades a year, 80% lost money (SEBI, FY23).

3 · Overconfidence

People think they know more than they do.

If everyone were rational, almost no one would trade (Milgrom & Stokey, 1982). People trade because they overrate their own information (Odean, 1998). Men traded 45% more than women, and lost more (Barber & Odean, 2001).

Before every trade, ask: who is on the other side, and why do I know better than them?

Synthesis10

Three studies, one idea

SEBI, 2023–2025

The problem

Nine in ten Indian F&O traders lose money, year after year, and most keep trading.

Sharpe, 1991

The arithmetic

Every trade has another side. Before costs, active investors earn the market on average. After costs, they earn less.

Barber & Odean, 2000

The reasons

Costs are certain, frequent trading multiplies them, and overconfidence keeps people trading.

Think about this: if professionals with better data, faster systems and lower costs are on the other side of your trade, what is your edge?
